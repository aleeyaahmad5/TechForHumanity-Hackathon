# 02 — Executive Summary

## Problem Statement

Framed from the participant's perspective, per Enterprise Design Thinking's "business problem → human problem" method (5 Whys):

> **Students in under-served communities — and the broader student population who want to serve them — lack access to mentorship, industry connections, and hands-on experience solving real health, education, housing, and accessibility problems. They have ideas and technical skill, but no low-barrier venue to test those ideas against real-world constraints, alongside people who've actually shipped products.**

This is *not* "we need to organize a hackathon." The hackathon is the solution; the problem it solves is a gap in access — to mentors, to industry, to a credible first project. Everything in this plan should be checked against whether it closes that gap for a participant like "Alex" (see [13_Participant_Storyboard](13_Participant_Storyboard.md)).

**Hill statement** (Who / What / Wow, per Enterprise Design Thinking):
*Who* — student participants in Melbourne, particularly those with limited existing access to industry networks. *What* — will be able to pitch a working social-impact solution to real industry judges and leave with a mentor relationship, not just a certificate. *Wow* — a weekend event, run by student interns with TI MedTech backing (as judge and Day 1 virtual workshop host) and Leon Gouletsas' strategic advice.

## Success Metrics — HEART Framework

| Dimension | Goal | Signal | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H**appiness | Participants feel the event was worth their weekend | Post-event survey | NPS ≥ 40; satisfaction ≥ 4/5 average |
| **E**ngagement | Participants actively use mentor time and workshops, not just code alone | Sign-in sheets, mentor logs | ≥ 70% of teams attend ≥ 2 mentor office-hour slots |
| **A**doption | Participants apply what they learned / activate their new network after the event | Post-event survey, LinkedIn connections | ≥ 30% report a new mentor/peer connection kept after 2 weeks |
| **R**etention | Participants want to return or stay involved | Follow-up survey, mailing list opt-in | ≥ 25% opt into future TI MedTech / RMIT events |
| **T**ask Success | Teams actually finish something judgeable | Submission count, judge scores | ≥ 80% of registered teams submit a working demo |

## Recommended Event Dates

**Melbourne, RMIT Activator: Saturday 21 – Sunday 22 November 2026 (2-day format).**

| Format | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-day (Sat–Sun) | Lower venue/catering cost (1 fewer day); avoids Friday class/work conflicts for volunteers and participants; matches a 2-person intern organizing team's realistic staffing capacity; still enough runway for a scoped MVP given a tight, well-run mentor schedule | Less total build time than 3-day; tighter judging turnaround | **Recommended** |
| 3-day (Fri–Sun) | More build time; room for an evening keynote/mixer on Day 1 | Volunteers/mentors likely to be unavailable Friday daytime (classes, work); one more day of venue/catering cost; higher fatigue risk for a first-year, intern-run event | Not pursued — TI MedTech's confirmed scope (judge + Day 1 workshop only) doesn't add staffing capacity, so the 2-day format stands |

Late November gives a **~14.5-week runway** from today (2026-08-10) to event day — enough time for the WBS in [03_Project_Plan_WBS](03_Project_Plan_WBS.md).

## Top Risks (full register: [11_Risk_Register](11_Risk_Register.md))

1. **Low participant registration** → weaker demo day energy. No longer a solvency risk — even the low end of the 50–150 total-head range (see [05_Budget_Revenue](05_Budget_Revenue.md)) is profitable on ticket sales alone, since venue/AV/volunteer costs are $0 and catering scales with headcount. *Mitigation:* start marketing in Week 2–3 (not Week 4+), lean on university clubs for distribution, keep the registration form to 5 fields.
2. **RMIT venue unavailable** → forces a late scramble for a backup space. *Mitigation:* secure a backup venue option by Week 3; confirm room/AV/cost directly with Prof. Michael Cowling early.
3. **WiFi failure during the event** → disrupts hacking, registration, and judging. *Mitigation:* stress-test WiFi in Week 14, arrange a backup mobile hotspot, keep an offline registration fallback.
