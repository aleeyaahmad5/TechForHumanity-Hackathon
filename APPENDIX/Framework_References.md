## Enterprise Design Thinking

**Hills (Who/What/Wow):**
> "Hills are statements of intent (the underlying purpose or goal) written as user outcomes. They follow a format of Who, What, and Wow: Who: Who are your users? ... What: What will your users be able to do that they couldn't before? ... Wow: What differentiates you from the competition?" (line 34–37)

Applied in [02_Executive_Summary](../02_Executive_Summary.md) — the Hill statement frames the event's purpose before any logistics.

**Problem Statements:**
> "Our users struggle to achieve some task today because of blocks, limitations, or other issues... Problem statements should always include a user and their current problem, not yours." (line 148–151)

Applied in [02_Executive_Summary](../02_Executive_Summary.md) — the participant-centered problem statement, contrasted against the organizer-centered framing the brief explicitly warned against.

**Restless Reinvention:**
> "View everything as a prototype... This mode of working enables more regular delivery of value to users." (line 87–88)

Applied as the rationale for treating this entire plan as a first-year draft to revise via [12_Evaluation_PostEvent](../12_Evaluation_PostEvent.md) lessons-learned, not a fixed blueprint.

**Diverse Empowered Teams:**
> "Diverse teams have varied perspectives, skills, and backgrounds... Empowered teams have the agency to make everyday operational decisions on their own." (line 106–107)

Applied in the [07_Volunteer_Staffing](../07_Volunteer_Staffing.md) role design and the [09_Participant_Experience](../09_Participant_Experience.md) skill-based team formation recommendation.

## UX Design

**HEART Framework:**
> "HEART stands for Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, and Task success. For each area, UX designers write the main goal for their product, specify the research method that indicates if they're on track for their goal, and state the metric that measures this goal." (line 242)

Applied directly in [02_Executive_Summary](../02_Executive_Summary.md) success metrics and [09_Survey_Template](../09_Survey_Template.md).

**User Journey Mapping:**
> "User journey mapping is a visual or graphic representation that maps a user's relationship with an organization, service, product, or brand across various channels and over time. It tracks a user's progress through various stages, starting from awareness to all the way to advocacy." (line 225)

Applied in [09_Journey_Map](../09_Journey_Map.md) and [13_Participant_Storyboard](../13_Participant_Storyboard.md), using awareness → registration → event → advocacy as the stage structure.

## Project Management

**Stakeholder Management:**
> "Internal stakeholders are individuals or groups directly involved in the planning, execution, and management of a project... External stakeholders are individuals or groups outside the immediate project team or organization. They have an interest in the project's outcome but are not directly involved in its daily activities." (line 33–37)

Applied in distinguishing internal stakeholders (Kashaf, Aleeya, Leon) from external ones (TI MedTech, sponsors, RMIT facilities) throughout the WBS owner assignments in [03_Project_Plan_WBS](../03_Project_Plan_WBS.md).

**Work Breakdown & Risk Management:** general WBS structuring (task, owner, dependency, milestone) and risk register format (likelihood × impact × mitigation × owner × trigger) follow standard PM practice as covered in the notes' project lifecycle sections — applied in [03_Project_Plan_WBS](../03_Project_Plan_WBS.md) and [11_Risk_Register](../11_Risk_Register.md).
